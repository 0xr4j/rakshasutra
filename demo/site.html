A thread through the crowd.

A child lets go of a hand on a mountain path with ten million pilgrims a year. Phones are in a locker, the network is down in the valley, and the path forks twice. Rakshasutra is a wristband that announces itself, and poles that listen.

The problem

Four ordinary facts defeat every tracker you can buy.

01

No usable network

Cellular coverage congests in festivals and hides behind ridges. Consumer Bluetooth tags need a stranger's phone and its data connection to report anything.

02

Cards see only gates

The passive RFID card issued at registration answers within 1 to 3 m of a turnstile. Between two gates, kilometres apart, it is silent.

03

Steel rain shelters

Long stretches of path run under galvanised iron sheds. A grounded steel roof shields satellites from below and wristbands from above.

04

No phones at the sanctum

Phones go into cloakrooms before the inner shrine. A found child cannot be reunited by calling a parent whose phone is in a locker.

How it works

A band that speaks, poles that listen, a server that knows the map.

The wristband does not know where it is. Every 800 ms it broadcasts fifteen bytes: who I am for this quarter of an hour, my battery, whether my button is pressed, and a signature.

Wristband

Anonymous to a stranger

The identifier and radio address are AES-CMAC outputs that rotate each slot. Without the key, one slot cannot be linked to the next.

Pole

Says it once

A pole under the roof hears a band a hundred times a minute and reports its arrival once, in chunks of 25, plus a 64-byte Bloom filter of who is still there. It holds no keys.

Radio

Both sides of the roof

Bluetooth listens below the steel; the LoRa antenna is led through it. The split pole avoids {{GiBle}} dB of roof loss on one link and {{GiLora}} dB on the other. design values

Server

Distance along the track

Poles 173 and 174 are consecutive numbers and {{BranchExample}} km apart on foot. The server measures separation on the route graph and alerts at 200 m.

The tether's scan

The parent tag listens for 5 ms every 5 s and alarms after four misses.

A 5 ms window hears the band {{TetherHit}}% of the time. The tag would be in false alarm {{TetherAlarmSim}}% of the time. It needs {{TetherWindow}} ms, or synchronised scanning.

The cold buzzer

A 100 µF capacitor holds the supply at 2.68 V during a 32 mA pulse at −5 °C.

Its time constant is {{TauMs}} ms against a 100 ms pulse. The supply falls to {{DroopCap}} V and the chip resets. It needs {{CapNeed}} µF, or a pulse under {{PulseMax}} mA.

Departure lists

15.8% frame loss at 3,000 wristbands; airtime cut by 82.4%.

{{LossRak}}% as specified. Departure lists cost more airtime than the heartbeat saves; without them, {{LossRakNd}}% and a cut of {{AirRedNd}}%.

Replay

Replayed frames are rejected.

Alarms are signed and verified. Ordinary sightings are not, and can be replayed inside their 45-minute slot window. The server flags impossible speeds.
